## Why I built it

This began as an assignment for my Film Appreciation class: analyse how colour is used in a film. Since I am a Computer Science student, a traditional report did not feel like the most honest way for me to approach it. I wanted to turn the analysis into something I genuinely enjoy making—an interactive experience with design, motion, and a little experimentation.

The result is *The Colour of Excess*, a visual essay about *The Wolf of Wall Street*. It follows Jordan Belfort's story scene by scene and looks at how colour supports ambition, persuasion, excess, and the gradual loss of control.

## What I built

- Fourteen scene readings that connect colour choices to the film's character arc.
- Interactive scene studies with measured palettes and focused visual notes.
- A motion-led opening, sliding hero, thumbnail controls, and asymmetric gallery.
- A downloadable PDF that preserves the analysis beyond the website.
- A companion video section that connects the fictional performance to the real Jordan Belfort.

## Design direction

The interface borrows from editorial print design: narrow display type, warm paper tones, dark brown, and flashes of orange. Motion helps the experience feel theatrical without burying the analysis. The goal was to make the assignment feel like an authored object rather than a report placed inside a website template.

## Built with

- Next.js 16 and React 19
- TypeScript
- GSAP animation
- jsPDF and HTML-to-image
- Responsive layouts and browser print fallbacks

## Current status

The interactive essay is live at [film.omartaheri.com](https://film.omartaheri.com/). The website, scene studies, colour palettes, and reviewed PDF are complete; film clips remain represented by clearly labelled local stills where licensed video is not available.
